  How to use Edlink Offline Workspace

  You can record daily attendance and enter draft marks without internet, then sync them to Edlink later.

  1. Prepare while online
     Sign in to Edlink and open Offline workspace. On your first visit, create a PIN with at least 6 characters. Remember it—you’ll use it to unlock
     your downloaded work.

  2. Download the work you need
     Choose Daily attendance or Draft marks. Select the class and attendance date, or the exam paper, then click Download. Repeat for each register or
     paper you need.

  3. Work without internet
     Open the downloaded item under On this device and enter attendance or marks. Your changes save on that device. You can reopen /offline/ in the
     same browser and device and unlock it with your PIN.

  4. Sync when internet returns
     Open and unlock the workspace. It syncs automatically while open and connected, or you can click Sync now. Wait until it says All saved changes
     are synced. If your login expired, sign in again using the same account and school.

  5. Review any conflicts
     If someone changed the same records online, click Review online changes. Choose whether to keep the online value or your offline value, then save
     your choices and retry.

  Remember:

  - Download everything you need before losing internet, including each attendance date.
  - Marks sync as drafts. Submit them for approval through the normal online marks page.
  - Click Lock when leaving a shared device.
  - Don’t clear browser data while changes are pending—you could lose unsynced work.
  - Your offline PIN is separate from your Edlink password and cannot be recovered.
 
 ////////exam work flow

  Here’s the flow:

  1. Set up categories and weights in System Settings → Academic → Examination
     categories. Add the assessment periods your school uses, such as Beginning
     of Term, Midterm, and End of Term. Their weights must total 100%.

  2. Create exams in Exam Setup. If there’s one category, it’s assigned
     automatically. If there are several, choose the category for the exam.
     Existing exams can be categorized from the exam list.

  3. Add subject papers to each exam, setting the maximum score and any paper
     weighting.

  4. Teachers enter marks, save them as drafts, and submit them for approval. An
     authorized staff member approves each paper.

  5. Review and publish results after the papers are approved and the
     publication checklist is satisfied.

  How the final subject mark is calculated: Each score is first converted to a
  percentage: score ÷ maximum score × 100. If a category contains multiple
  exams, their percentages are averaged. Then each category average is
  multiplied by its configured weight, and the weighted results are added. For
  example, category averages of 60% at 20%, 70% at 30%, and 80% at 50% produce a
  final mark of 72%.

  If a required category has no approved marks, the school’s existing missing
  assessment rule applies. With the default “incomplete” rule, the subject
  result is marked incomplete until all categories have marks.


Think of an exam as an assessment sitting, and an exam paper as the subject-
  specific part students take.

  For example, a school configures these categories in System Settings:

   Category             Share of final mark
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Beginning of term                    20%
  ───────────────────  ─────────────────────
   Midterm                              30%
  ───────────────────  ─────────────────────
   End of term                          50%

  Then in Exam Setup, the school creates an exam called Midterm Assessment for
  Primary 4 and assigns it to the Midterm category. The exam name describes the
  assessment event; it isn’t the subject.

  Next, the school adds papers to that exam:

   Paper subject    Maximum score
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Science                    100
  ───────────────  ───────────────
   Mathematics                100
  ───────────────  ───────────────
   English                    100

  Teachers enter each learner’s score for each paper. So a learner might get
  70/100 in Science in this Midterm Assessment.

  If the learner’s Science scores are 60% in Beginning of term, 70% in Midterm,
  and 80% in End of term, the final Science mark is:

  (60 × 20%) + (70 × 30%) + (80 × 50%) = 72%

  The paper weighting field is an additional multiplier for papers within an
  exam, for cases where one paper should count more than another. Most schools
  can leave it at 1. As I mentioned, it isn’t currently included in the new
  category based term calculation, so that field’s effect needs refinement.
